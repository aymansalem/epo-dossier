1. Executive Summary & Legal Framework

This observation presents concrete technical prior art under Article 115 EPC demonstrating that the split-stay accommodation relocation workflow recited in EP4523100 was publicly disclosed, technically implemented, and operationally used by "Hotels-Plus" in 2019[cite: 1]—more than three years prior to the applicant's priority date of 10 May 2022[cite: 1].

The submitted evidence specifically targets the amended claim set filed on 17 June 2025 (CLMS-1.pdf). Airbnb's amendments introduced a dual-condition trigger in Claim 1 requiring both: (i) a length-of-stay parameter exceeding a minimum threshold[cite: 1], and (ii) an available inventory count failing to satisfy a minimum inventory threshold[cite: 1].

Core Grounding of Invention Defect:
  • Lack of Novelty (Art. 54(2) EPC): Document D-02 ("Hotels-Plus") explicitly discloses both threshold evaluations within its backend logic (check_availability.php)[cite: 1] and admin control interface, directly anticipating the structural limitations of amended Claim 1[cite: 1].
  • Lack of Inventive Step (Art. 56 EPC / COMVIK Principle): Document D-01 (KAYAK / US2018330283A1) establishes that split-stay matching architectures were state-of-the-art prior to 2018. Under T 641/00 (COMVIK), combining inventory count thresholds with stay-length checks constitutes a non-technical business parameter executed via standard computer logic.

3. Technical Sequence Mapping (Hotels-Plus vs. Claim 1)

The Hotels-Plus evidence documents an identical, automated multi-day accommodation processing sequence[cite: 1]:

  1. Accommodation Request Intake: The user submits a search specifying check-in date, check-out date, and total length of stay (e.g., 1–10 Feb)[cite: 1].
  2. Stay Processing & Threshold Check: The system evaluates whether the requested stay duration transgresses a defined length-of-stay threshold (check_availability.php)[cite: 1].
  3. Continuous-Stay Search & Unavailability Trigger: The database checks single-property availability[cite: 1]. When continuous availability is insufficient (failing an inventory threshold), the relocation/split-stay generator is invoked[cite: 1].
  4. Partitioning of Stay: The engine splits the requested multi-day stay into successive segments (e.g., 4 nights in Standard Room + 6 nights in Family Room; or Segment 1 in Hotel A + Segment 2 in Hotel B)[cite: 1].
  5. Combined Result Compilation & Presentation: The system compiles the split segments into a unified itinerary and presents the ranked combined listing in the user interface[cite: 1].

5. Inventive Step Attack under Article 56 EPC (COMVIK Principle)

In the event the applicant argues that the specific conjunction of evaluating length-of-stay thresholds alongside inventory counts is not identically disclosed in a single reference, amended Claim 1 remains unpatentable under Article 56 EPC:

Application of T 641/00 (COMVIK) to Amended Claim 1:

  1. Non-Technical Character of Thresholds: Setting a minimum length-of-stay threshold (e.g., 5 nights) or an inventory threshold is a purely commercial/administrative business decision regarding inventory allocation[cite: 1].
  2. Lack of Technical Synergy: Executing two standard logical conditional statements (IF stay > min_stay AND IF inventory < min_inventory) within a web database search produces no unexpected technical effect across memory, processor, or network hardware.
  3. Obvious Implementation: A software engineer seeking to optimize accommodation search results given the non-technical business requirement of offering split stays when inventory is low would implement this dual conditional trigger as a routine programming step.
